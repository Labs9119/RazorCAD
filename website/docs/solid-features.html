User manual / Solid Features

Solid Features

Live previews

While a feature's Property panel is open, the model is rolled back to just before that feature and the feature is shown in colour: yellow for material it adds and red (seen through the body) for material it removes. Fillets and chamfers show the material they add or remove; reference geometry being edited is drawn in orange. Click ✔ (or Enter) to finish.

Every value field accepts numbers or equations (see Equations in the Evaluate chapter).

Extrude and Extrude Cut

Profiles uses the same canvas selector as Loft. Click inside a closed region or on its contour to add it; click it again or use its remove button to deselect it. Select several coplanar profiles to extrude them together, including profiles from different sketches. Clicking a sketch in the tree selects its whole profile, including text and generated outlines. Picking an individual region afterwards replaces that whole-sketch selection. Nested regions can be selected separately, so you can extrude a ring or just the disk inside its hole.

Model faces as profiles: click a flat face of the model (where no sketch profile is under the cursor) to use the face itself as a profile, holes and all, without drawing a sketch on it; click it again to remove it. A face profile lies in the face's plane and extrudes out of its body (tick Reverse direction to go into it, for a cut). It can be combined with sketch profiles in the same plane and takes every end condition, the draft and every operation; it cannot be a Thin Feature. If the face changes when earlier features change, the profile follows it.

Chain selection selects connected curves. Turn it off to pick individual segments, then choose Add selected contour or press Enter; Escape clears the unfinished selection. Open contours enable Thin Feature. Turn off Select profiles in canvas to pick end references or target bodies; choosing an up-to end condition also switches to reference picking. Existing whole-sketch extrusions continue to build as before.

Extrudes a sketch profile into a solid, or cuts it out.

Start and End are independent. Start from → Profile plane preserves the usual extrusion. Offset from profile translates the starting plane by a signed distance along the first selected profile's normal; expressions are supported. Plane / planar face projects the profiles along the extrusion direction onto the underlying plane of the selected reference, including inclined planes. Use Pick start surface in canvas or the Start surface list. End references have their own picker and do not replace the start reference.

Blind depth, symmetric and two-sided distances are measured from the new start. Reversing the extrusion changes its extent direction while preserving the start offset. With an inclined start and a distance end, both caps follow the starting plane, separated along the extrusion direction. Up To Surface, Offset From Surface and Up To Vertex retain independent end references. Crossing boundaries, zero thickness and a starting plane parallel to the extrusion direction are rejected. The options apply to solid extrusions, cuts, Thin Feature and selected profile regions; saved start-plane references follow source edits. Surface Extrude retains its existing start behavior.

End conditions:

End conditionMeaning
BlindA set depth
Mid PlaneThe total depth split equally on both sides of the sketch
Two sidesIndependent Depth and Second depth on opposite sides of the profile plane
Through All / Through All – BothThrough every body, in one or both directions
Up To VertexClick a model vertex or choose a reference point
Up To SurfaceClick a flat model face or choose a plane; angled surfaces are supported and the extrusion is trimmed to the surface
Offset From SurfaceAs Up To Surface, at a set distance from it

Second Side: with a one-sided end condition (Blind, Through All, Up To Vertex, Up To Surface or Offset From Surface), tick Second side to extrude the other way from the start as well, to an end of its own: Blind (its own Depth), Through All, Up To Vertex, Up To Surface or Offset From Surface, with its own vertex or surface picker (Pick second side surface in canvas). The second side always goes the opposite way to the first: an end of the second side that lies on the first side's side of the profile is refused. Both sides share the start, the operation and the Thin Feature; the draft is shared too (each side drafted away from the profile) unless Second side drafted on its own is ticked (see Draft). Mid Plane, Two sides and Through All – Both already reach both ways and do not offer it.

Options:

  • Operation: Join merges with existing material where possible; New body keeps the result separate; Cut removes material; Intersect keeps only the material inside the extrusion. A Join that does not touch existing material makes a new body. An Intersect changes each body the extrusion overlaps and leaves the others as they are; with Feature Scope limited to chosen bodies, a chosen body it does not overlap is removed. An Intersect that overlaps no body is refused, and an Intersect feature cannot be patterned or mirrored.
  • Thin Feature: makes a wall of the given thickness from the profile; direction one side, other side or mid-plane. Open sketches switch it on automatically.
  • Draft: tilts the walls by the Draft angle, about the plane the extrusion starts from: the solid narrows away from the profile, or widens with Draft outward. The walls of holes in the profile tilt the other way with them, so the material keeps one taper (a cut narrows as it goes in). Mid Plane, Two sides, Through All – Both and a second side draft each side away from the profile. Up To and Offset From an angled surface take the draft too: the walls tilt about the profile and the angled end is trimmed with them. From an angled start surface the walls tilt about the start surface, so the profile's outline is kept where the extrusion starts. If the walls would meet before the end, the draft is refused. With a second side, or with Two sides, Second side drafted on its own gives the second side (the Second depth) a Second side draft angle of its own, inward or (Second side drafted outward) outward; 0 leaves it straight.
  • Feature Scope (cuts and intersects): All bodies by default; untick it and click the bodies to cut or intersect.

Revolve and Revolve Cut

Profiles uses the common canvas selector: choose regions, connected contours or individual entities rather than automatically consuming a whole multi-profile sketch. A flat model face can be a profile too, as for Extrude; its axis must then be a reference axis, a model edge or face, or a line of a sketch (a face has no sketch axes). Coplanar selections can be combined. Whole-sketch selection remains available explicitly and older files remain compatible.

Choose Select axis in canvas separately from the profiles. The axis may be a sketch axis, a line in an earlier sketch (including construction geometry), a reference axis, a straight/circular model edge or a cylindrical face. Project axis into profile plane projects its origin and direction onto the selected profile plane; a perpendicular axis cannot be projected. Return to Select profiles in canvas to edit the profile selection.

Extent is Full (360°), Angle, Up To Vertex or Up To Surface. Up To Vertex turns the profile round the axis until it reaches the vertex (click a model vertex, or choose a reference point); Up To Surface until it reaches a plane or flat face. A plane through the axis stops the revolution at whichever half of it comes first; another plane or face trims the revolution where it crosses it, keeping what lies on the profile's side. Reverse direction turns the other way round. A vertex on the axis, a surface through the profile and a surface the revolution never reaches are refused (a profile touching the axis needs an end plane through the axis). Surface Revolve goes up to them too. Angle supports One side, Two sides with independent angles, or Symmetric (the total angle split equally). Reverse exchanges the signed direction for one/two sides. The total angle must not exceed 360°. Profiles must not cross a coplanar rotation axis. Full ignores stored angle values. Older files whose angles were clamped to 360° keep that geometry and migrate to Full when edited.

Operation offers Join, New body, Cut or Intersect (as for Extrude; Sweep and Loft offer the same). Thin Feature revolves a wall along open or closed contours; open contours enable it automatically. Cuts have the same Feature Scope as Extrude; Select target bodies in canvas switches explicitly from profile/axis selection to target-body picking.

Sweep and Sweep Cut

Sweeps a profile along a path.

  • Profile: a selected sketch region or contour, or Circular with a diameter (useful for springs).
  • Path: a selected sketch chain, 3D sketch, curve or helix; or, under Path from Model Edges → Select model edges, edges of the model joined end to end into one chain (open or closed, without branches). A chain of model edges is followed from its end in the profile's plane.
  • Orientation: Perpendicular to path keeps the section square to the path; Parallel to initial profile moves it along the path without turning it (tight bends can make the sweep invalid); Square to faces it runs on keeps the profile's plane through the normal of the faces chosen with Select faces in canvas, for a path that lies on them (a groove round a curved part).
  • Twist angle (with Perpendicular): the profile turns by this total angle along the path, by distance: Twist → Evenly along the path, or Eased in and out (slowly at both ends, fastest half way). Positive angles turn by the right-hand rule along the path. Equations are allowed; 0 is the usual sweep. A twist needs an open path with no sharp corners (round them first), and the profile in the plane square to the path at its start.
  • End scale (without a guide): the profile grows or shrinks about the path from its own size at the start to End scale times it at the end, by the length along the path, evenly or (Scale → Eased in and out) slowly at both ends; 1 keeps its size. It works with every orientation and with a twist. Checked against the volumes integrated along a line and an arc to 1e-7.
  • Guide (optional): a second curve (sketch chain, 3D sketch, curve or helix) the profile follows. It replaces Orientation and Twist; removing it brings them back.
    • Guide controls → Orientation: the profile turns to keep facing the guide and keeps its size. The guide doesn't have to touch the profile.
    • Guide controls → Section size: the profile is scaled evenly so its edge stays on the guide. The guide starts on the profile's edge and crosses each plane square to the path once.
    • A guided sweep takes one closed profile without holes or Thin, an open path and an open guide. Both curves run between the planes square to the path at its ends, and don't touch. A guide drawn the other way round is turned automatically. Contact with the guide is checked to 0.0001 mm; geometry that doesn't fit gives an error rather than an ordinary sweep.
  • Thin option (without a guide).

Select Profile / Path / Guide in canvas activates one role at a time. Click inside a closed profile or on its contour; click path and guide curves directly. Each role has its own Chain selection checkbox. Turn it off to pick individual connected entities from one sketch, then choose Use selected contour or press Enter. Add or clear an unfinished contour before switching roles. Paths and guides may include construction curves. Different contours from the same sketch can be used in different roles, but the same entities cannot be reused across roles. Unrelated sketch geometry is ignored by selected contour references; missing selected curves report an error. Other sources (whole sketch / curve) retains explicit whole-sketch and supported derived-curve selection, including existing files.

Solid Sweep uses the same Join / New body / Cut operation control as Extrude and Revolve. Cut supports explicit target-body selection. Without a guide, selected regions retain internal holes and open profiles can use Thin Feature.

Loft and Loft Cut

Blends between two or more section profiles, in order. A third or later profile shapes the middle.

Activate Select profiles in canvas, then click a sketch contour or inside a closed region. Multiple regions in one sketch can be selected independently; holes belong to their enclosing region. Select guides in canvas switches to guide selection, including construction curves. Source sketches are temporarily shown while selecting, without changing their saved visibility.

Chain selection picks connected curves automatically. Turn it off to select individual segments from one sketch, then choose Add selected contour or press Enter. For a sweep's path or guide, tick From one segment to another as well: click the first segment and then the last, and the segments between are added (the rest of the sketch is left out). Escape clears an unfinished selection. Profiles and guides have separate ordered lists with move and remove controls, matching numbered highlights in the canvas. Other sources retains whole-sketch and supported 3D curve selection. Stored contour references follow source curve IDs through edits and renaming; missing or ambiguous boundaries report an error. Surface Loft uses the same contour selection for open or closed sections.

Guide contacts are checked against the actual CAD profiles. The fit uses the original guide curves and refines intermediate sections until sampled distances to the CAD faces are within 0.01 mm. If this check fails, the feature reports an error: add a section near a tight bend. These are sampled numerical checks, not a guarantee over every point of the continuous curve. Thin lofts retain the wall's offset from the sketched guide.

Guide construction → CAD constraints (two profiles) builds patches constrained directly by the original 3D guide edges. It checks the kernel's contact error and independent distances against 0.0001 mm. This method currently requires two profiles without Thin Feature, with guides starting and ending on corresponding patches. Holes in the profiles are lofted between them without the guides (each hole's sections joined directly) and cut out of the guided outline; they must stay inside it. Across guide seams selects C0 (contact, the default), G1 (tangent joins) or G2 (curvature-continuous joins along shared guides). Original profile corners are preserved. Unsupported or incompatible constraints report an error without falling back to approximation. Adaptive approximation remains available for the broader set of loft options and is the default for existing features.

Profile correspondence

Enable Edit profile connectors to display numbered connections between matching profile vertices. Use Pick start in canvas, choose a start vertex in the panel, or drag a connector endpoint to another existing vertex on that profile. Moving an attachment shifts the correspondence around the contour while preserving boundary order. For circles, set the seam angle (expressions are supported) or drag the seam in the canvas. Open surface profiles use their endpoints; reversing their direction exchanges the endpoints.

Settings follow the selected profile through renaming, reordering, save/reopen and source edits. A deleted attachment reports an error and must be reselected. Use a profile's Automatic for this profile button to clear its settings, or reset all connectors to return to automatic matching. Closed profiles must share the same traversal direction: reversing only some would fold the loft. Manual matching is passed to the CAD builder without automatic remapping and also applies when guides or endpoint conditions are used.

Manual connectors support sketch profiles, and in a Loft 3D sketch profiles too (their points, or a circle's seam angle; not a start inside a curve). Start vertex → Inside a curve… (or dragging a connector onto a curve between vertices) starts the profile at a point inside one of its curves, Along it a share of the curve's length in its own direction (expressions allowed): the profile gains a point there, without splitting the sketch, so a triangle can be lofted to a square by starting the triangle inside one of its edges. Every profile must end up with the same number of points.

Profiles with holes list each hole: Hole 1, Hole 2… choose which of this profile's holes goes with the other profiles' hole of that number (pair them by hand when the automatic pairing joins the wrong ones), and Hole … start sets where each hole starts (a vertex, inside a curve, or a seam angle for a circle), as for the outline.

Thin lofts take the connectors too: each wall starts at its vertex nearest the profile's start. A thin loft with a 3D sketch profile is the solid loft's walls (made square to its sides, sharp at its corners), so its connectors work as a solid loft's. Other 3D curve profiles use automatic matching. More connectors: after a profile's start vertex, Add connector adds another vertex to pair (Connector 2, 3…), so profiles with different numbers of corners can be matched corner to corner as you choose (a square's four corners to four of a hexagon's six). Every profile needs the same number of connectors, in the same order round it; between two connectors, the profile with fewer sides has its longest ones halved until the sides pair one to one. Connectors in a different order round one profile would make the loft's sides cross, and are refused. Surface Loft uses the same controls.

Start and end conditions

Start Condition and End Condition control the first and last section independently:

  • Free preserves the usual loft when both ends are free.
  • Normal to profile makes the longitudinal endpoint derivative normal to its planar sketch.
  • Direction uses a world axis or a reference axis. Create a reference axis from a sketch line for an inclined direction.
  • Tangent to adjacent faces (G1) matches the tangent planes of existing solid or surface faces along the endpoint profile. Click Select start support faces or Select end support faces, then click the adjacent side faces in the canvas. Select all faces needed to cover the outer and inner profile edges, including Thin walls: one cylindrical face can cover a circle; a rectangular extrusion needs its four side faces. For a hollow section also select the adjacent inner walls. The profile must coincide with their boundary, not merely lie inside a face. Click Select profiles in canvas to return to contour selection.

Curvature to adjacent faces (G2) also matches the curvature of the selected adjacent faces. Set this independently at either end, including G2 at both ends or a G1/G2 combination. It uses the same support-face selection rules as G1. Curvature is measured on the CAD surfaces, including the along-edge, across-edge and mixed components; incompatible geometry reports an error. A sampled CAD normal check also rejects detected local collapses between profiles; reduce Influence or add a section if this happens. This does not certify the absence of every possible self-intersection.

Influence is a positive multiplier of the endpoint derivative length; 1 uses the distance to the neighboring profile as the scale. Larger values extend the endpoint's influence farther into the loft. Expressions are supported. Reverse tangent direction reverses the signed derivative and may create an invalid or overlapping loft.

Endpoint conditions require matching profile curve counts and a smooth loft. Solid lofts support internal holes and Thin Feature, including Outside, Inside and Mid-plane sketch offsets and open Thin profiles. Every section must have the same number of corresponding inner loops. Outer and inner walls are constructed together and sewn to end faces with openings, rather than filling the cavities. G1 and G2 support selection must cover all walls. They support two or more sections, and closed profiles for solids or open/closed profiles for surfaces. Intermediate sections use C2 cubic interpolation. Normal to profile requires a planar sketch; Direction also supports curve sources in Surface Loft. Closed surface profiles stay uncapped.

Guide curves with endpoint conditions use Adaptive approximation. Guides must meet every section in order, and their endpoint tangents must agree with the requested tangent planes and direction. Conflicting constraints report an error. Refinement uses 8/16/32/64/128 intermediate stations per profile interval and checks independent native guide points against the final CAD side faces to 0.0001 mm. With Thin, guide checks retain the sketched outline’s offset to the outer wall; inner loops follow the guide deformation. Multiple guide deformations blend smoothly between contact locations (C2 blend weights), preserving physical profile corners. Influence remains relative to the original neighboring section, independently of the refinement count. CAD constraints with C0/G1/G2 guide seams still requires free endpoint conditions; selecting it with endpoint constraints gives an explicit error instead of switching methods.

The kernel constructs clamped tensor B-splines (cubic, or degree five along the loft when G2 is requested), refining profile interpolation from 8 through 128 intervals. It checks off-node profile contact and endpoint derivatives, including influence and direction. Final sewn CAD faces are checked again at 33 positions per endpoint for contact within 0.0001 mm, tangent-plane mismatch within 0.5°, and relative endpoint derivative error within 0.0001. These are sampled numerical checks, not a certified global bound. Unsupported or incompatible constraints report an error without dropping the endpoint conditions. G2 additionally compares all three components of the second fundamental form on the final CAD faces, with curvature mismatch at most 0.001 1/mm. Feature notes report measured contact, angular and G2 curvature errors.

  • A circle is split into as many equal arcs as the other profile has corners.
  • Profiles with different segment counts give an error naming the sketches.
  • Options: straight (ruled) sides, Thin. Without straight sides: Through the profiles curvature-continuous (C2) or tangent-continuous (C1), and Smooth the surface, which evens out its bends.
  • Guide Curves (optional): curves the loft's outline follows between the profiles, such as a body's outline seen from the side. Each runs from the first profile to the last, through every profile, and is a selected chain of sketch curves or a supported curve source. Separate guides can come from the same sketch. One guide pulls the loft along it; two or more shape it round the outline, each where it touches the profiles. The profiles themselves are kept as drawn. Holes in the profiles, and a thin loft's inside, move with the outline, so a thin wall keeps its thickness; for a thin loft, draw the guide on the sketched line.

Hole

Hole (Features tab, Insert ▸ Features ▸ Hole…) makes standard holes.

TypeSizes
Hole (clearance)ISO 273 close, normal or loose fit, for M2 to M24
CounterboreFor socket head cap screws (ISO 4762)
CountersinkISO 10642, 90°
Tapped holeISO metric or Unified (inch), drilled at the tap drill size; the thread is shown as a cosmetic thread and given in drawing callouts
CounterdrillA wider drill (standard sizes: the counterbore's diameter and depth) with a drill point of its own angle down to the hole
SlotTwo half circles of the hole's diameter joined: Slot length between their centres, Slot angle from the face's X direction
Custom sizesYour own diameters and depths
  • End: Blind (with a Drill point of Drill point angle, 118° unless set), Through All, Up To Next (each hole until its axis first comes out of the material: through the wall it starts in), Up To Vertex, Up To Surface and Offset From Surface (a plane or flat face, picked in the canvas or chosen in the list; each hole's own depth along its axis); reversible.
  • Far-side countersink (Through All, Up To Next): a countersink of its own diameter and angle where the hole comes out (the far side of the first wall for Up To Next, of the last for Through All).
  • Place holes at clicked points on a face, or exactly at the points and circle centres of a sketch on the face. On a flat face each clicked hole shows its place as From left and From bottom: its distances from the face's left and bottom edges, looking at the face (its up the part's up). Type them to put the hole exactly there. Each click adds a hole, more than one on the same face too; a click on a hole already placed takes it away. A hole on a flat face keeps to the face as the part changes: when the face moves (a thicker plate) the hole starts on it where it is now, and when the face changes size (a dimension, a configuration) the hole stays as far from the face's nearer edges, across and along, as it was placed. Bosses, snap hooks, the 3D-printing holes and pockets keep to their faces the same way. On a curved face (a shaft, a pipe) a hole is drilled square to the face where it was clicked: a radial hole.
  • Holes work on sheet metal too, and a drawing's Hole Callout reads their size from the feature.

Helix

Pick a sketch (or a 3D sketch) with a circle; it sets the axis and diameter. Define the helix by pitch and revolutions, pitch and height, or height and revolutions. Options: start angle, clockwise, reverse. Use a helix as a sweep path for springs and coils.

Thread Cut and Thread Extrude

Click a bore (Thread Cut) or a shaft (Thread Extrude).

  • The size is chosen automatically from the diameter, or choose a metric (ISO) or inch (Unified) standard size, or custom. Coarse / UNC sizes are preferred.
  • Optional pitch; left-hand option.
  • Length: the whole face, a depth, or up to a plane or face. Threads never reach past the ends of their face.
  • The bore or shaft is resized to the thread size if needed (a bore to the minor diameter, a shaft to the major). A bore that would break out of the body is refused.
  • Threads run all the way to the ends: grooves run out into open air, otherwise the thread is cut off flush with the end.

Cosmetic Thread

Cosmetic Thread (Features tab, Insert ▸ Features ▸ Cosmetic Thread…) marks a bore or shaft as threaded without modelling the thread, so it costs nothing to rebuild. Use it where the thread only needs to be shown and called out.

  • Click the bore (internal thread) or the shaft (external thread). The thread starts at the end nearest the click; Start from the other end reverses it.
  • Size, pitch, left-hand and length (whole face, depth, or up to a plane or face) as for Thread Cut. The feature's note says when the face is not the size the thread expects (a bore about the minor diameter, a shaft the major).
  • In the model it shows as a thin helix on the face. Tapped holes made with the Hole feature show their threads the same way.
  • On drawings it is drawn by convention: from the side, thin lines at the thread's other diameter and a line where it ends, dashed for a hidden bore; end on, a three-quarter circle. A view's Cosmetic threads box turns them off.
  • Hole Callout on a bore with a cosmetic thread gives the thread, for example "M10×1.5 THRU ALL / TAP DRILL Ø8.5 THRU ALL".

Wrap (Emboss / Deboss)

Pick a sketch (outlines and/or text) and a face, flat or curved. The sketch is projected along its normal onto the face and raised (emboss) or sunk (deboss) by the depth.

Patterns and Mirror

Patterns and mirrors copy features or bodies. Any feature that changes the bodies at a place can be copied: extrudes, cuts, holes, fillets, chamfers, drafts, domes and sheet metal forms. A feature without a shape of its own (such as a fillet) is run again on the matching edges or faces of each copy.

FeatureCopies
Linear PatternAlong one or two directions (X, Y, Z or any reference axis)
Circular PatternRound an axis (X, Y, Z or any reference axis)
MirrorAbout a plane or flat face
Sketch Driven PatternAt every point (or circle centre) of a sketch
Curve Driven PatternAlong a sketch, 3D sketch or helix, spaced evenly to the end or at a set distance; moved, or turned to follow the curve
Table Driven PatternA copy for each row of a table: x, y from the feature along a plane's directions (the feature's own sketch plane when none is chosen) and optionally an angle turning the copy about the plane's normal through the feature. Rows are separated by ; or new lines, cells by commas, tabs or spaces, so a table pasted from a spreadsheet works (a header row is skipped); cells can be sums or use variables
Fill PatternOn a square or staggered grid over a flat face, kept a margin from its edges (for example a perforated panel)

Linear Pattern options : Distance → From the first to the last spreads the instances over a total length instead of setting the spacing between each two. Spacing grows by makes each spacing that much more than the one before (a negative value: less), for copies that spread out or close up. Symmetric adds as many again the other way from the feature. With a second direction, Pattern the feature only copies just the feature along it, not the whole first row (an L of copies).

Circular Pattern options : Angle grows by (without equal spacing) makes each angle between copies that much more than the one before. Symmetric adds as many again the other way round (less than a whole turn). Rings makes rings of copies further out from the axis, Ring spacing apart, out through the feature's middle.

Instances to Skip (Linear and Circular) : instance numbers to leave out, separated by commas: 1 is the feature, 2 the first copy, -2 the first copy the other way (symmetric). With a second direction or rings, row.column: 3.2 is the third along the first direction (or round) in the second row (or ring).

What to copy. Choose the Feature, and add any number of More features: each is copied the same way. In a Linear, Circular, Sketch Driven or Curve Driven Pattern, Clicking the model picks either Faces, where each face brings the feature that made it (click a hole's wall to copy the hole), or Bodies, which are copied whole to every place as new bodies. Mirror takes bodies too (tick them, or click them on the model). Fill Pattern copies features only.

Feature folders. Right-click a feature ▸ Add to New Folder… (or Move to a folder already there, or Remove from Folder) to group features in the tree; a folder sits where its first feature is, with its features under it. Right-click a folder to rename it, delete it (its features stay), or make a Linear or Circular Pattern of the Folder. A pattern's More features list takes folders too: it copies whatever is in the folder each time it rebuilds, so a feature added to the folder later is copied as well.

Library features. Right-click a feature or a feature folder ▸ Save as Library Feature… to keep it under a name in your settings, for any part: the feature with the sketches it uses (and, for a folder, everything in it), and the variables its sizes use. Features ▸ Library Feature puts it in the part you are in: choose it, set its variables (each copy gets variables of its own: a second copy's w becomes w_2), the angle to turn it about the face's normal, then click the plane or flat face for its first sketch. The copy goes into a folder of its name, and its features stay ordinary features you can edit. A feature that uses a plane, a body or projected geometry outside the group cannot be saved this way (put that in the group too).

Patterns of cuts are cut out in one operation, and patterns of added features (bosses, pins, ribs) are fused into the body in one operation, so large patterns rebuild quickly. A plate with 200 bosses builds in about 2 seconds.

A pattern or mirror of a sheet metal feature that makes a body (Base-Flange, SM-Extrude, Lofted-Bend, Transition) copies that body as it is at the pattern, with the flanges and cuts added to it so far. Each copy is sheet metal with its own flat pattern. A mirrored copy's flat pattern is the mirror image, with its bends going the other way. A mirrored lofted bend becomes a plain solid.

Fillet

Open the feature and click edges on the model. The model is rolled back to just before the feature so every edge can be picked; tick Preview to see the result. The radius accepts equations.

  • Edges are remembered by type, direction and position, and found again after parameter changes.
  • Where a fillet can't be added on top of earlier fillets (a corner where several blends meet), RazorCAD recomputes it together with the preceding fillets in one operation.
  • Where two blends exactly meet (for example R2 on both edges of a 4 mm wall) the result is a full round.
  • Variable size: a radius at the start and another at the end of each edge, or Radius ▸ At vertices and points along edges: the panel lists the vertices of the selected edges (the ends of each run of edges) with a radius for each; empty ones use the fillet's radius. Along each edge the radius changes from the one at its start to the one at its end.
  • Points along the edges (with radii at the vertices): Add Point, then choose the edge, where along it (the share of its length from its start in %, or a distance from its start in mm) and the radius there; or Add by Clicking and click on the edge where the point goes. The radius runs smoothly from the edge's start through its points to its end. While the fillet is edited each point is marked on its edge, so you can see which end the % counts from; drag a mark along its edge to move the point (its % or mm follows, and the fillet rebuilds when you let go).
  • Setback at corners: where three or more fillets meet, the corner is blended smoothly from this distance out, instead of the usual small corner patch. A little more than the radius works well; 0 keeps the usual corner. The fillet's note says how many corners were blended.
  • Face fillet: pick faces instead of edges; the edges where they meet are rounded.
  • Full round : click a side face, the face to round off, then the other side face (flat and parallel: a wall of even thickness). The middle face is replaced by one round meeting both sides, its radius half the wall's thickness.
  • Size by → Chord width (constant size) : the value is the width across each fillet, from edge to edge; each edge's radius follows from how far its two faces turn (measured at its middle: exact between flat faces).
  • Shape → Curvature continuous (G2) (constant size) : instead of a round (which meets the faces beside it in tangent but jumps from flat to curved), each blend leaves its faces with the same tangent plane and no curvature, then curves gradually: no visible line where it starts, as on styled housings and covers. It sets back from the edge as far as the round of that radius would, but stays closer to the sharp corner, so it takes less material away. The blend is exact (a curve of degree five swept along the edge, not a fitted patch). It is made on straight edges between flat faces whose ends meet flat faces, and on circular edges all the way round between a flat face and a round face on the same axis (the foot or top rim of a boss, a counterbore's edge: the section revolved round the axis); round corners and other curved edges in a fillet of their own. The note reports the tangent and curvature mismatch measured on the CAD faces. For a G2 patch over an opening use Filled Surface with Boundary continuity Curvature (G2); for G2 joins between lofted profiles use Loft → Across guide seams → G2.
  • An edge's own size : each edge in the list has a box for a radius of its own (expressions allowed); blank uses the fillet's. One feature can so round edges with different radii in one go, corners and all.
  • Add a face's edges: then click a face, and all its edges are added (clicked again, removed).

Chamfer

Click edges as for a fillet. Chamfer types: equal distance, distance + angle or two distances. Flip direction swaps the sides. With equal distances each edge can have a distance of its own, and Add a face's edges works as for Fillet.

Vertex (cut off a corner) : click corners of the model where three straight edges meet; each is cut off by the plane through the points the distance along each of its edges. Different distances along X, Y and Z gives the edges running most along X, Y and Z their own distances. A distance as long as its edge, or a corner in a recess (where the cut would add material), is refused.

Shell

Click the faces to remove and set the wall thickness, inward or outward. With no faces removed, the body is hollowed: a closed void inside, the thickness in from the outside everywhere (a body too thin for it is refused). Hollowed outward, the body itself becomes the void and the walls are built round it, the thickness out from every face (edges and corners rounded).

Walls of Their Own Thickness : Select walls in canvas, then click flat faces of the body: each wall gets a thickness of its own, more than the shell's (the box beside it). Inward, the material behind the face is kept down to that depth; outward, the face is built out to that thickness over its own outline. A removed face cannot have one (it is the opening). Shell validates the resulting solid and rejects a failed offset that merely returns the original full body. For inward shells, small fillets or chamfers can be retained outside with sharp corners inside; the feature note reports this construction. If no valid cavity can be made, reduce the thickness, select different removal faces, or place Shell before the fillets and chamfers.

Draft

Pick a neutral face, then the faces to tilt by an angle. Reverse tilts them the other way.

Rib

An open sketch line is thickened and filled out until it meets the model, either in the sketch plane (for example a gusset across a bracket corner) or square to it.

Dome

A spherical cap or half ellipsoid on round faces, or a smooth dome filled over any other flat outline. Reverse presses it in.

Plastic part features

Four features for moulded parts (Features tab).

  • Mounting Boss : posts at clicked points on a flat face (or at a sketch's points and circle centres), rising out of the face: Outer diameter, Height, a Draft on the outside, a Hole (diameter, and a depth: empty for the full height), and Ribs: how many, their width, how far out from the boss and how high.
  • Snap Hook : a cantilever beam (length, thickness, width) with a hook at its tip: its overhang, its lead-in angle from the beam (the slope it slides in on) and its catch angle (90°: square, holding; less: it lets go when pulled). Hook faces turns it about the face's normal.
  • Lip / Groove : click the flat rim face along the open edge of a shelled part: a lip (raised) or groove (sunk), along its inner or outer edge, of a width and height (depth). For two halves that fit: a lip on one, on the other a groove a little wider and deeper.
  • Vent : slots across a sketch's closed outline on a face, of a slot width with bars between them, at an angle, kept a margin in from the outline, cut through or to a depth.

Core and Cavity

Core and Cavity (Features tab) makes the two halves of a simple mould for the part : a block round it (Block margin beyond it every way), the part taken out of it grown by the Shrinkage (the plastic shrinks as it cools), split at a parting plane square to the Pull direction. The plane goes at the part's largest section along the pull (or At a height up the part). The half the pull direction points into is the Cavity, the other the Core; holes along the pull become shut-offs where the plane crosses them. Parting can also be a parting surface of your own (a surface body reaching past the block all round, made with the surface tools along the part's parting line): the block is split by it, the side the pull points into the cavity. Check the part with Draft Analysis and Undercut Check first.

Side Core (after Core and Cavity) makes a side action for an undercut: click its faces on the part (a side hole, a catch) and the direction it is pulled (the first face's normal, or an axis). A block round the faces (grown by the margin), from the part out past the mould block, is taken out of the core and the cavity and made a body of its own, Side core.

Sprue and Runners cuts round runners along the lines of a sketch on the parting plane (half in the core, half in the cavity; they gate into the part where they meet it) and a tapered sprue from the back of the cavity down to the start of the first line.

Move Face

Push or pull flat faces along their normal, or turn a face about one of its edges. The neighbouring faces follow.

Delete or Resize Fillet, Defeature

These edit a solid directly, with no history needed: good for imported parts.

Delete / Resize Fillet (Surfaces tab): click fillet or chamfer faces. Delete carries the faces either side on until they meet at a sharp edge again; Resize then rounds (or chamfers) that edge at the New radius. The faces round them are grown or shrunk to close the gap exactly, so it works on most rounds and chamfers, including rounds that run into other rounds at a corner; the message says what the faces were (R2, chamfered, blended). When the faces cannot close the gap, it falls back to the simpler way: rounds along straight edges between flat faces (convex or concave), full rounds on circular edges (between a flat face and a round face, like the foot or top rim of a boss) and chamfers between flat faces, whose ends meet flat faces.

Defeature takes every fillet up to a radius and every hole up to a diameter out of a body (or of every solid), for a simpler part to send to a supplier or to analyse. A hole is filled with its counterbore, countersink and drill point; one whose counterbore is wider than the limit stays whole. They are all taken out at once, exactly, the faces round them grown or shrunk to close the gaps (the message then says "taken out exactly"). If the kernel cannot close a gap, the body is done the simpler way, one at a time, and fillets whose ends do not meet flat faces, and holes that break out of the side of the part, stay; the message says how many were taken out.

Scale

Scales every body by one factor, about the origin or about the centre of the bodies.

Long operations

If a feature takes more than a few seconds, a banner above the model says what is being worked on ("Still working on Extrude Cut1 (12 s)…") with a Stop button (also next to the progress bar). Stop restarts the geometry kernel: a feature you are still editing is cancelled and a finished feature is suppressed, so you can unsuppress it to try again.
