User manual / Bodies and Multi-Body Parts

Bodies and Multi-Body Parts

Solid bodies

A part can contain several solid bodies. A boss that doesn't touch the existing material makes a new body; untick Merge result to keep a touching boss separate. A cut that slices a body in two leaves two bodies.

The Solid Bodies (n) folder at the top of the tree lists the bodies:

  • Click a body to select and highlight it.
  • Double-click (or right-click ▸ Rename) to name it. Unnamed bodies are named after the last feature that changed them.
  • Bodies are identified by the feature that created them, so names and materials stay attached when you change dimensions.
  • Folders: right-click ▸ Add to New Folder or Move to folder to organise bodies.
  • Identical bodies are listed once with their count (×N). Open the item to see each body. Right-click for Isolate, Hide or Show all of them. Identical means the same volume, surface area, principal moments of inertia, handedness and material (and for sheet metal, the same thickness and flat pattern).

Body Properties

Right-click a body (tree or view) ▸ Body Properties…. It holds the body's name, material, description and any number of custom properties (name/value pairs such as part number or finish). It also shows the body's mass, volume, area and centre of mass. For sheet metal bodies it adds the sheet data (Sheet Metal chapter).

Materials

The Material item in the tree sets the part material (Tools ▸ Edit Material…). A body set to Use part material follows it. A body given its own material keeps it when the part material changes and is drawn in that material's colour. Mass Properties adds up every body with its own density. The material list is in Appendix B.

Appearances

The Appearances tab (above the feature tree, next to Configurations) holds the colours and finishes put on the part: on single faces, on whole bodies, or on the whole part. Use them to tell parts and bodies apart at a glance, or to set a part up for a photorealistic render.

Adding one. Select faces or a body (or nothing, for the whole part) and click + Add Appearance in the tab (or Add Appearance on the Render tab, or right-click a face ▸ Face Appearance… / Part Appearance…, or a body ▸ Body Appearance…). The panel shows:

  • Apply to: Faces, Bodies or Whole part. With Faces or Bodies, click them in the view to add them; click one again to take it off.
  • Appearance: the library, by category. Metals (machined, mill-scale, brushed and polished stainless, chrome, galvanised, cast iron, brushed, bead-blasted and cast aluminium, checker plate, brass, copper, bronze, titanium, gold, weathering steel); Anodised aluminium (clear, black, red, blue, gold, green, purple, orange, titanium grey); Paints and coatings (gloss, satin and matte paint, metallic paint, smooth and textured powder coat, hammertone, safety yellow, red oxide primer, a plain colour); Plastics (gloss, matte, textured, nylon, clear acrylic, frosted); Glass (clear, tinted, frosted); Timber (oak, pine, walnut, maple, cherry, birch plywood, MDF); Stone and concrete (concrete, polished granite, marble, slate, sandstone, brick); Rubber (black, grey, red, ribbed matting, translucent silicone); Meshes and gratings (bar grating, expanded metal, perforated sheet with round holes or slots, woven wire mesh, welded wire fence mesh, chain-link fence); Fabrics and composites (carbon fibre twill, canvas, leather, felt).
  • Colour: any colour over the look (a red anodised, a green powder coat); Reset goes back to the library's colour.
  • Texture (looks with a pattern): its Scale and Angle. Patterns are drawn at their real size (a bar grating's bars 30 mm apart, a brick 215 mm long) and laid on each face from the side it faces most, so nothing needs unwrapping.
  • Name: shown in the tab (empty: the look's name).

✔ keeps it; ✗ cancels (an edit goes back to what it was).

Meshes and gratings are see-through : a plate given bar grating, expanded metal, perforated sheet or a fence mesh shows its holes (and casts the matching shadow in renders) without them being modelled. The part stays light and quick to rebuild, and the face can still be picked, measured and sketched on.

Order matters . The tab lists the appearances top to bottom in the order they apply; each goes over those above it. A face coloured red and, lower down, its body given oak shows oak; drag the oak above the red (or right-click ▸ Move Up) and the face is red again. The first row, Materials and body colours, is what is under them all: the bodies' materials and colours.

In the tab click an appearance to see what it is on, double-click it to edit it, drag it to change the order, and right-click it to Rename, Suppress (it stops applying but is kept), Copy, Move Up / Down or Delete it. Undo and Redo take them back like any change. Faces are found again by where they are when the model changes; if a face can no longer be found the appearance shows ! (hover for how many), and editing it lets you pick again.

Appearances are saved with the part and go with it into assemblies (as colours: textures show in the part itself). Render looks from Render (below) still apply to faces without an appearance. The older per-face colours of earlier versions still show, under the appearances.

The Render tab

The Render tab has the rendering tools: Render, Quick Render, Realistic View, Lighting, Add Appearance, Appearances (the tab), Edit Material, Decal, Decals and Export glTF (for other viewers and renderers).

Realistic View shows the part in the view as it renders: every face physically based (metals reflect, glass is clear, textures and gratings as they are), lit by the render scene. Click it again to go back to the normal shaded view. Lighting sets the scene (below), the exposure, the floor's shadow and how far a photographed place is turned, for renders and the realistic view; it is kept with the part. Quick Render renders the view at its size in one pass.

Render makes a photorealistic picture of the view as it is now : choose the size (as the view, or up to 3840 × 2160), the Quality (Draft: one pass; Good: 16 passes, Best: 64, each with the view and the light moved a little and the passes averaged, for smooth edges and soft shadows), the Scene, a soft shadow on the floor and the exposure, and a look for each body: polished, brushed or cast metal, gloss paint, powder coat, glossy or matte plastic, rubber or glass, or From the material (aluminium and stainless brushed, steel cast, brass and copper polished, ABS glossy, PLA and nylon matte). Bodies keep their colours, and their looks are kept with them; faces and bodies with an appearance render with it (its texture, finish and holes). Save PNG… writes the picture. The settings are kept with the part.

The scenes light the part, show in its reflections and sit behind it:

The last nine are photographed places : real 360° HDR photographs. The place lights the part (its sun, sky, windows and lamps as bright as they really are), shows in its reflections, and is the backdrop. The part stands on the place's ground: the photograph's ground carries on under the part, seen from as high as the view looks from, with the part's shadow and a soft contact shadow where it meets the floor. So the view's angle matters: looking down on the part shows mostly the ground (a concrete floor, grass, paving); a lower view shows more of the place behind it. A perspective view (View ▸ Perspective) suits them best.

The sun (or the brightest light) is put at the view's right, where the key light is, so the part's light, its shadow and the place agree. Lighting ▸ Turn the place (degrees) turns the place about the vertical to choose what is behind the part; the sun, the key light and the shadow turn with it. Where the photograph's ground is not flat (hedges, the water's edge), turn the place until the part stands on open ground. The photographs are from Poly Haven (CC0); app/scenes/CREDITS.md lists them.

Spotlights

Render ▸ Spotlights (or Add Spotlight, or + Spotlight in the Appearances tab) places lights of your own around the part, as in a photo studio: a key light, a fill, a rim light, a coloured accent. They light renders and the realistic view only; the ordinary view is not changed. While the Spotlights panel is open the realistic view is on, and each light shines on the part as you change or drag it.

  • In the view: each light shows as a yellow lamp, a ring where it is aimed, and its beam's cone. Drag a lamp to move the light across the screen; Shift+drag moves it nearer to or farther from what it points at, along its beam. Drag the ring onto the part to aim the light at that point of its surface (off the part, the ring moves across the screen).
  • In the panel: the list of lights (click one to edit it; + Add Spotlight, Delete), and for the light picked: its Name, On, Colour (with warm, neutral, daylight and coloured presets), Brightness, Cone angle (how wide the beam is), Soft edge (a hard-edged or soft-edged pool of light), Casts shadows, and where it is (Light at) and where it points (Aimed at) as X, Y, Z. Aim at the Part points it at the part's middle; Light from the View puts it where the view looks from, pointing the same way.
  • Scene lighting: the scene (Lighting…) still lights the part. Turn its Scene light down and its Scene key light off to light the part with the spotlights alone, as in a dark studio; the Dark studio scene suits this.

✔ keeps the lights (with the part; Undo takes them back), ✗ puts them back as they were. The Appearances tab lists them under the appearances: double-click one to edit, right-click to turn it off or delete it. In multi-pass renders each spotlight's shadows are softened, more for a softer edge.

Render ▸ Decal puts a picture (a logo, a label) on a flat face: open the picture, give its width and angle, then click the face where its middle goes. Decals show in the view and in renders, are saved with the part, and are not geometry. Tools ▸ Decals… lists them, to delete them.

Exploded views of a part

A part with two or more bodies can have exploded views: Insert ▸ Exploded View…, or right-click the Solid Bodies folder. Only the display moves; the model stays as it is. The steps are made as for an assembly (see Exploded views in the Assemblies chapter).

Combine and Subtract Bodies

Combine joins bodies with Add, Subtract or Common; the first body clicked is the main body. Subtract Bodies picks a cutting body and target body; Consume cutting body (on by default) removes the cutting body afterwards.

Split

Cuts bodies into separate bodies with a plane, a flat face or a surface body. With a plane or face you can keep all the pieces or just the piece on one side.

Move/Copy Bodies

Moves or copies solid and surface bodies.

  • By coordinates: ΔX/ΔY/ΔZ plus a rotation about X/Y/Z or a reference axis, through the axis or the bodies' centre. Copies repeat the step.
  • With mates: Coincident, Distance, Parallel, Perpendicular, Angle and Concentric between an entity of the moving bodies (face, edge, vertex) and fixed geometry (another body, a plane, axis or point). Flip alignment turns a mate around.

Delete Body

Removes a body from the model from that point in the history. Deleting the Delete Body feature brings the body back.
