User manual / Fast Structure

Fast Structure

The Fast Structure tab builds welded frames (weldments) from standard steel profiles: Structural Member, Trim/Extend, End Cap, Gusset, Weld Bead and Cut List. It also has a 3D Sketch button, since frames are often laid out in 3D.

Structural Member

Pick a profile and a sketch or 3D sketch: every line and arc of it becomes a member, each its own body named after its profile. Add more sketches to the Path list for a frame in 3D, for example the top of a table and its legs; the joints between them are treated too. Arcs, and the bends of a 3D sketch with a bend radius, become bent members.

ProfileTypes
Hollow sectionsSquare (SHS), rectangular (RHS), circular (CHS / pipe)
AnglesEqual and unequal angle
Channels and beamsChannel (PFC / UPN); I-beam (UB, UC, IPE, HEA)
BarsFlat bar, round bar

Sizes come from AS/NZS or EN/ISO tables, or enter your own dimensions.

  • Corners where two members meet end to end: Mitre, Butt (first or second member trimmed) or None (members meet at the sketch points). Trimmed ends: square cut on the face, or coped to the other member's outside.
  • A member ending on the middle of another (a T-joint), or under a corner (a table leg), is trimmed to it, square on its face. Pipes and round bars are always coped (fish-mouthed).
  • Alignment: which point of the profile the sketch line runs through (centre, corners or edges), a profile rotation, and mirror.

Trim/Extend

Click the members to trim on the model, then the boundary: a plane or another body. Each member is trimmed (or, with Extend members that stop short of it, extended) at its end nearer the boundary, either cut square on the boundary's face or coped to the boundary body.

End Cap

Click the open end faces of tubes or pipes. A plate of the set thickness is added on the end (outside) or in the end (flush).

Gusset

Click two flat faces that meet at a corner, one on each member. A triangular plate fills the corner. Set the two leg lengths, the thickness, a corner chamfer for weld clearance, and where the plate sits along the corner (centred or to one side, plus an offset).

End caps and gussets are bodies of their own, named by size for the cut list.

Weld Bead

Set the weld size and click the bodies to weld (none: every weldment member). A bead runs along every line where two of them touch; each joint is its own bead body.

Cut List

Cut List lists identical members together, with their profile, length, end angles, quantity, total length and mass (from the bodies' materials), then plates and welds (with the total weld length). It also gives the total length of each profile. Export CSV… saves it for a spreadsheet. A drawing can show it as a Cut List Table (Drawings chapter).

Duct Fitting

Duct Fitting (Weldments tab) makes a sheet metal duct fitting of outside sizes and a sheet thickness: a segmented elbow (angle, centre line radius, number of gores: two end gores and the rest full ones), a cone (reducer: the other diameter and the length), a round to rectangle transition, an offset (two parallel round ends side by side) or a lateral (a branch joining a main duct at an angle). Each is a body at the origin (move it with Move Body). Its flat patterns are developed on the middle of the sheet, with a seam allowance along the long seam: the message lists them and their sizes, and File ▸ Export ▸ Duct Patterns (DXF)… writes every fitting's patterns side by side, named with their quantities. Elbow gores are worked out exactly; transitions and offsets are unfolded by triangles, every edge at its true length.

Pipe Route and Cable

Pipe Route runs a pipe along the straight lines of a sketch or 3D sketch. Where a line branches square off a straight run, an equal tee goes in (ASME B16.9 sizes); other branches and crosses are reported. Choose a size (DN15 to DN300, Schedule 40 walls) or a tube of your own outside diameter and wall. Corners: Elbows puts a long radius elbow at each corner (1.5 × the nominal size, or the Bend radius you give), each elbow a body of its own; Bends makes one bent tube (3 × its diameter, or your radius). Reducer puts a concentric reducer (to a smaller size, its B16.9 length) at the first end, the last end (in the order the lines were drawn) or both. Flanges at the (other) ends adds a flange ring at each open end without a reducer. The straight pipes are weldment members, so the Cut List gives their lengths; the message sums them up. A line too short for the elbows at its ends is reported.

Spool Drawing (Weldments tab, in a part with a pipe route) makes a drawing with one isometric view of the pipework, the cut list of the straight pipes and a FITTINGS table (each elbow, tee, reducer and flange, and how many).

Cable sweeps a round cable or bundle along a sketch or 3D sketch (a 3D sketch spline through the points it must pass follows them smoothly). Give its Diameter, or the number of Wires and their diameter (the bundle is about 1.15 × d × √n across). The message gives the route length and the cut length with the Slack added.

Harness Formboard lays the part's cables flat at their cut lengths, joined where their ends meet: each cable straight on from the one before it, branches fanned out at 45° steps. It shows the board with each cable's name and length and the points where cables meet, and Export DXF… writes it to lay out the board full size.
