Standard views

View menu: Front (Ctrl+1), Back (Ctrl+2), Left (Ctrl+3), Right (Ctrl+4), Top (Ctrl+5), Bottom (Ctrl+6), Isometric (Ctrl+7) and Trimetric. Normal To (Ctrl+8) looks straight at the selected face or plane.

Up axis: Y or Z

A part can be shown Y up (the default) or Z up: View ▸ Up Axis, right-click Origin in the feature tree, or click Y up / Z up at the right of the status bar. With Z up the Top plane is XY, the Front plane XZ and the Right plane YZ, and Z points up; the tree shows each standard plane's axes beside its name. The choice is kept with the part and used for new parts too.

Only the names of the axes change: the model stays where it is and no feature moves. The triad, X / Y / Z directions, Move / Copy Bodies, reference point coordinates, Measure, Mass Properties and 3D sketch coordinates all use the part's axes, and so do exported STEP, STL, OBJ and 3MF files: a part shown Z up is written Z up, and a file imported into it is read Z up. The origin and corner triad always show X in red, Y in green and Z in blue: the vertical axis is green with Y up and blue with Z up.

An assembly keeps its own up axis in the same way (View ▸ Up Axis, the status bar, or right-click its Origin in the tree; Undo takes it back). Its tree shows the standard planes' axes, and a STEP file exported from an assembly shown Z up is written Z up. A new assembly starts with the up axis used for new parts.

Display styles

View menu or the heads-up toolbar: Shaded With Edges, Shaded, Hidden Lines Removed and Wireframe.

Perspective (the heads-up toolbar's box button, or View ▸ Perspective / Orthographic, also in the Display Style list) draws the view in perspective: nearer things look bigger, as a camera sees them. Rotating, panning, zooming (in perspective it moves the view in and out), picking and dragging work as before, and renders are taken in perspective too. Orthographic (the default) draws without perspective, so parallel edges stay parallel. The choice is kept in your settings for every part and assembly.

Visibility

  • View ▸ Show / Hide Planes toggles the standard planes.
  • The eye column at the right of the Feature tree shows or hides each sketch, plane, axis, point, curve, body and body-making feature on its own. Hiding a feature hides the bodies it built. Planes, axes, points and curves can also be hidden one at a time with right-click ▸ Hide.
  • The eye button on the heads-up toolbar: click it to hide everything ticked in its list at once, and click again to show it all again (the ticks are kept); right-click it (or View ▸ Visibility…) for the list: standard planes, reference planes, sketches, construction lines, axes, points and surfaces, with select-all and deselect-all.
  • Construction lines : a sketch's construction lines show with the sketch. Once a feature uses a sketch it is hidden, and its construction lines with it; tick Construction lines in the eye's list to show every sketch's construction lines even so (to pick one as a revolve's axis, say). Right-click a sketch ▸ Show Construction Lines / Hide Construction Lines sets it for that sketch alone, whatever the list says.
  • Right-click a body (tree or view) ▸ Hide. Tab hides the body under the cursor; Shift+Tab shows the hidden body under the cursor.
  • View ▸ Show Hidden Bodies shows hidden bodies so you can click them to show them again. Choose Done Showing Hidden Bodies to finish.
  • Right-click a body ▸ Isolate hides everything else. The isolated body can still be edited. View ▸ Exit Isolate returns.
  • Right-click a body, or any feature that made or changed it, ▸ Make Transparent / Make Opaque. Transparency is saved with the part and can be undone.

Section view

Tools ▸ Section View… (or the Evaluate tab) cuts the model open on a plane so you can see inside. Choose the plane, an offset (equations are allowed) and flip the side kept. With a flat face, a plane or a sketch selected when you start, the cut starts on it; while the panel is open, click a flat face or a plane to cut from it instead. Drag the blue arrow on the cut to move it along its normal (the offset follows, in round steps; looking straight along it, drag up to bring it toward you). Tick Section 2 (and Section 3) to cut with two or three planes at once: only what all of them cut away is removed, e.g. a quarter cut. The arrow and a click in the view move the section you changed last. It works the same in assemblies (Evaluate ▸ Section View): every component is cut and capped, and a click on a component's flat face cuts from it. ✓ keeps the cut on while you work; the Section View button then opens the panel again, and ✗ removes the cut. Saved with a cut on, a part or assembly opens with it again, and each tab keeps its own cut. Each cut body or component gets its own cap in its colour, outlined and hatched with section lines; the hatch angle and spacing change from body to body so touching parts read apart. Section View can't be used inside a sketch.
