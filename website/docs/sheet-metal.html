User manual / Sheet Metal

Sheet Metal

The Sheet Metal tab holds the sheet metal features. Sheet metal bodies are listed in the Sheet Metal Bodies folder below Solid Bodies, with sheet metal icons.

Flanges and bendsEdge Flange, Miter Flange, Hem, Sketched Bend, Jog

Base Flange

A closed sketch (holes allowed) becomes a sheet of the given thickness. The bend radius and K-factor set here become the part's defaults.

Bend calculation (Base Flange, Sheet Metal Extrude, Lofted Bend, Transition): K-factor, or, from your bend table for this thickness and radius, the Bend allowance (the length a 90° bend takes flat) or the Bend deduction (how much shorter the flat is than the two outside lengths to the sharp, for a 90° bend). RazorCAD works out the K-factor it gives (refused when outside 0 to 1), so every flat length agrees with the table at 90° and other angles follow in proportion. Bend table (by angle) takes the allowance for several angles ("30: 1.5; 45: 2.0; 90: 4.2", for this thickness and the default radius): each bend's allowance is read from it, straight between its rows and in proportion past its ends; bends of another radius use the K-factor its 90° allowance gives.

Gauge tables

Base Flange, Sheet Metal Extrude and Transition / Reducer have a Gauge list: mild steel, steel gauges, stainless 304, and aluminium 5052 and 6061-T6. Picking a gauge sets the thickness, inside bend radius and K-factor. Save… keeps your own values as a gauge.

Sheet Metal Extrude

Extrudes an open sketch of lines and arcs into a sheet metal part. Arcs in the sketch are bends of their own radius; sharp corners get a bend of the inside radius set in the panel. Material goes on one side, the other side or both sides of the sketch; depth one way, reversed or mid-plane. A circle on its own is rolled into a cylinder, leaving a seam gap. Flanges, hems, Flatten and DXF export work on it.

Lofted Bend

A sheet metal part between two open profile sketches (each one chain of lines and arcs), for example a round-to-square transition, hopper or duct piece. Points are matched along both profiles by length, keeping every corner of either profile.

  • Method: Bent makes flat facets folded along straight lines, each fold a bend line in the flat pattern and DXF; set the number of divisions. Formed makes a smooth sheet (rolled or pressed), with the set number of forming lines along the profiles.
  • The flat pattern is developed at the neutral layer (K-factor) and works with Flatten, DXF export and flat pattern drawings. Material on the other side flips the thickness.
  • A cut through a lofted bend is carried into its flat pattern, and the cut edges are square to the sheet, as a laser-cut blank formed to shape would be. This makes trimmed conical parts possible: loft the cone between two arcs, then cut it to the outline with a revolved cut.
  • Edge flanges, corner reliefs and closed corners can't be added to a lofted bend; its shape comes from the profiles.

Transition / Reducer

Cones, concentric and eccentric reducers, square-, rectangle-, triangle-, pentagon- and hexagon-to-round transitions (either way up) and polygon frustums.

  • Sizes, offset and rotation are set in the panel; sizes can be inside, outside or mean. A sketch line from bottom centre to top centre places the part and sets its height; change the line and the part follows. Started with no sketch, it asks for a plane and opens the Line tool.
  • The part is made from a number of equal pieces, each a sheet metal body with its own flat pattern: a cone from one piece or more, a flat-sided transition from two or more, with seams at the middle of the flat sides or at the corners.
  • Formed (rolled, with a set number of forming lines per corner or round the whole part) or bent (bump formed with a set number of bends).

Edge Flange

Click straight edges on the top or bottom face.

SettingOptions
LengthMeasured to the outer virtual sharp, inner virtual sharp or tangent
Flange widthAlong the edge: the whole edge, Offsets from its ends (from the start, from the end; Swap the ends), or A width in its middle; the rest of the edge stays as it is, with reliefs where the bend ends
AngleFlange angle
PositionMaterial inside, material outside, bend outside, plus an offset; flip
Bend radiusIts own radius, or the default
Auto reliefRectangular, obround or tear relief where a bend ends next to material
CornersFlanges picked together that meet at a corner: Corner to corner (the default: each runs on up to the other's inside face and ends square, their inside edges the gap apart; the outside corner is left open and the bends end in a V cutout), Mitred (they meet on the diagonal), Overlap (one runs on to cover the other's end) or Open. Closed corners keep a gap (default 0.5 mm). For Overlap, Swap reverses which covers, and the Bend region is a V cutout or Closed.

Miter Flange

A flange with a profile of up to four segments along a chain of edges. Each segment has a length (to the outside of the bends) and an angle; a negative angle folds back the other way, so 90 / 90 makes a C and 90 / −90 a Z. The corners are mitred (closed, with a gap) at every level. First bend sets where the material sits on the edge, as for an edge flange.

Hem

Folds a straight edge back on itself. Hems meeting at a corner are mitred.

SettingOptions
TypeClosed (pressed flat), Open (with a gap), Teardrop (its own angle, default 225°) or Rolled (a curl)
LengthFrom the outside of the fold
Gap, inside radius, angleAs the type needs
PositionInside (the fold at the edge) or Outside (added beyond the edge); flip direction

Sketched Bend

Draw lines on the top or bottom face of a flat part of the sheet; the sheet is bent along each one. Set the angle, the radius (or the part's), whether the bend is centred on the line or starts or ends at it, the fold direction and which side stays put. The flat pattern stays the plate it was.

Jog

One sketch line on a face makes two opposite bends that step the sheet over by a height. The height is measured face to face, outside to outside or inside to inside; the jog says when the height is too small for the bends.

Cross Break

Click flat faces to mark an X of shallow stiffening folds. It shows in Flatten, in the DXF (on the Forming marks layer, with a note) and in flat pattern drawings.

Tab and Slot

Click the edge of one sheet metal part where it sits on another, then the face of the other part. Tabs are added along the edge and matching slots, with clearance all round, are cut through the other part, so the parts locate each other for welding.

  • Settings: number of tabs, tab length, tab height (empty: through the other part), margin at each end (empty: evenly spread) and clearance.
  • Both flat patterns and DXFs get the tabs and slots.

Forming Tools

Forming Tools presses a form into the sheet. Place forms at clicked points, or exactly at the points and circle centres of a sketch on the face (for a louver, its lines). They are pressed toward the face they are placed on, or the other way.

FormSettings
DimpleDiameter, depth
Extruded (flanged) holeHole diameter inside the collar, collar height, bend radius
LouverWidth back from the slit, opening height, hood side; drawn as a line on the face (the slit)
BeadA round-topped stiffening rib along each sketch line, its width and height (at most half its width), its ends rounded
LanceA bridge: slits along the sketch line and its width from it, the strip between pressed out by the height and ramped back at 45° at its ends; both slits are cut lines in the flat pattern
EmbossEach closed outline of the sketch raised (or pressed, with Flip) by the height, with straight walls the sheet's thickness; its outline is marked in the flat pattern

The flat pattern gets a centre mark and a note for each form, an extruded hole's pre-hole (the collar laid flat) and a louver's slit as a cut. Forms stay with the part through later sheet metal features and are left off, rather than breaking the part, if one can't be made.

Gusset

Click a bend (its round face) to press a stiffening dart across it at that point. Set the size of each leg from the inside corner and the width across the bend; tick Pressed down to press it the other way. The flat pattern marks it. (For gusset plates between structural members, see the Fast Structure chapter.)

Corner Relief

Adds a rectangular, circular or tear relief at every corner where two bends meet.

Closed Corner

Closes the corners between 90° flanges: one flange covers the other's end, with a gap.

  • Corner type: Overlap, Underlap, Butt (square ends: each flange runs on up to the other's inside face, the gap between their inside edges, the bends in a V cutout) or Mitre (the gap across the mitre, bends included).
  • Which corners: click a face of a flange next to a corner and the matching flange is found automatically; the clicked flange is the one that overlaps or tucks under. With no corners picked, every corner of the body is closed.
  • Swap reverses which flange covers.
  • Bend region: V cutout (default) runs each bend into the corner as it would form from the flat, leaving a small teardrop-shaped opening with a relief at the corner point. Closed keeps the bends meeting on the diagonal.
  • The flat pattern matches the folded part at closed corners, including each bend's run-on, the relief and the corner round.

Unbend and Bend

Unbend keeps the picked face in place and flattens the rest. Features after it (such as cuts across bends) are made in the flat. Bend folds it back to where it was. Both appear in the tree.

Cuts and other features on sheet metal

Cuts on sheet metal (extruded cut and so on) become holes in the flat pattern, so the flat pattern always matches. Holes (the Hole feature) work on sheet metal too. Other features that change a sheet metal body are allowed, but the flat pattern can't follow them; this is shown in Body Properties and the DXF preview.

Flatten

Click Flatten on the tab, or right-click the body ▸ View Flat Pattern. The flat pattern is shown on its own; Esc or Flatten again restores the view as it was. While it is shown, Export DXF… on the flat pattern banner (or right-click the view) exports it straight away.

Convert to Sheet Metal

Turns an ordinary body into sheet metal. Pick a flat face of a thin-walled body (for example a STEP export of a sheet metal part, or a shelled box), or of a knitted surface of flat faces (enter the thickness).

  • RazorCAD follows that side of the sheet across every smooth edge, through flat faces and cylindrical bend faces, so the whole body is found. Each bend keeps its own radius and angle.
  • Bends that run straight into each other with no flat between (rolled shapes such as a fan scroll) are joined automatically. Cut-outs in bends are kept and holes stay true circles in the DXF.
  • Sharp-cornered bodies (shelled boxes) get bends of the chosen radius at every edge between two flat faces.
  • A flange whose ends run right up against the material beside it, with no relief (a flange off a cross bar between the sides of a window, or off the edges of a slot), is ripped there: the flat pattern (and its DXF) has a cut line along each flange end, across the bend and on to the flange's edge, on the cut-outs layer, as the part is cut and bent.
  • The panel shows what was found in the body, for example "thickness 3 mm · bend radii R300, R227, R13 (inside, 3 bends)" or "4 sharp edges".
  • Use the body's thickness and Use the body's bend radii are on by default. Untick either to set your own value (pre-filled with the detected one).
  • A body curved other than in plain bends (a square-to-round transition or a reducer from another program, a cone) is unrolled: laid out flat at the neutral layer, triangle by triangle, so its flat pattern keeps every length; a ring is cut open at a seam. For a cone, click its curved face. A closed tube (bends all the way round) is unrolled the same way, cut open at a seam. A face curved both ways (a dished end) can't be laid flat and is refused. Cuts in such a sheet are unrolled with it (the hole is in the flat pattern); flanges, hems and corners can't be added (as on a lofted bend).
  • A body that RazorCAD folded itself (for example its STEP export) gets its closed corners back as RazorCAD made them: the corner type (V cutout, closed mitre or butt), which flange covers which and the gap are worked out from the body, and the relief and curved V lines are made again exactly. Other corners are traced from the body as they are.
  • After converting, the sheet's volume is compared with the body's. The feature note warns when they differ by more than 0.5 % (for example a nut or motor part that isn't sheet metal).
  • A curved surface that is curved one way only (a cylinder, or a profile swept straight) is converted into a rolled sheet: its section is followed by smoothly joined arcs, each a bend of its own radius. Arc fit tolerance (default 0.05 mm) sets how closely it follows the surface. Thickness goes behind the surface, in front of it, or both sides.
  • Trim where the sheet would run into itself (on by default) trims a rolled sheet back where its two ends would meet, leaving a small clearance.

Etch Lines

Sheet Metal ▸ Etch Lines: pick a sketch drawn on the top or bottom face of a flat part of the sheet. Its lines, arcs, circles, splines and text are marked in the flat pattern, for etching, scribing or marking part numbers and bend marks. They go on the DXF's ETCH layer (DXF Settings can leave them out) and show in flat-pattern drawing views (View Properties ▸ Etch lines), clipped to the face they are on. The folded part is not changed; the etch lines show on the model as thin lines.

Sheet Metal Check

Sheet Metal Check lists every bend of the sheet metal body: direction, angle, inside radius, length and the straight flange on each side. It warns about flanges shorter than 3 × thickness past their bend, and holes closer than 2 × thickness + radius to a bend.

Sheet metal Body Properties

Body Properties for a sheet metal body shows thickness, bend radius and K-factor, number of bends, overall flat size, flat area, mass and quantity, plus:

  • Cut length: the total length of the flat outline plus every hole and cut-out, and the number of pierces (closed contours), for laser-cutting estimates. Also shown in the DXF part information and under flat pattern drawing views.
  • Flat pattern seen from: top face or bottom face. The bottom face mirrors the pattern and swaps UP / DOWN bend lines and notes. Flatten, DXF export and drawing views all follow it.

Exporting flat patterns as DXF

  • Right-click a sheet metal body (tree or view) ▸ Export Flat Pattern as DXF, or Export DXF on the tab.
  • Export All DXF: choose a target folder; one file per body, named after the body. Identical bodies give one file with the quantity multiplied by the count.
  • Nest Flat Patterns : lays every sheet metal body's flat pattern out on stock sheets (width × height, a margin round the sheet, a gap between parts, parts turned 90° where that fits better). Identical bodies are counted, times each body's quantity and the number of Sets. Parts are placed by their true outlines (the default: bottom left first, the biggest first, into each other's gaps and inside the holes of others) or by the rectangles round them (fast). They can be turned not at all, by 90° steps, or (by outlines) by 45° or 15° steps. The result shows each sheet and how much of it is used; Export DXF… writes one DXF with the sheets side by side, their outlines on layer SHEET and each part's outline and holes, named at its middle.
  • Each file is shown in a preview window with its layer colours and line types, which can be changed there. Buttons: Export, Skip This One and Abort Export.
  • In the preview, zoom with the wheel, pan by dragging and double-click or Fit to see it all. Click lines to select them (Ctrl or Shift adds, Shift+drag for a box, Ctrl+A for all), then Delete them, give them their own Line type, or move them to another Layer (for example make a line an etch line). Undo and Restore All put them back. These edits apply to that one export.
  • Options: bend lines, bend notes, bend region (hidden) lines, etch lines and part information.

DXF Settings

DXF Settings on the Sheet Metal tab sets the defaults for every export: layer mapping (colours and line types) and options. Defaults: up bends red, down bends blue, etch lines yellow dashed. Reset to Defaults restores them.
