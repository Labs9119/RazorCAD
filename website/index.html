Parametric 3D CAD for Windows. Free and open source.

Sketch with constraints, build parts feature by feature, put them together in assemblies and make drawings. RazorCAD runs offline on the OpenCascade kernel, and it’s yours to use, share and change.

Sketches that hold their shape

Draw lines, arcs, circles, slots and splines, then pin them down with relations and dimensions. A constraint solver keeps everything in place. Geometry turns from blue to black once it’s fully defined, and the Property panel tells you how many degrees of freedom are left.

  • Dimension, with equations and global variables in any value
  • Trim, offset, mirror, pattern and convert model edges into the sketch
  • Import DXF files straight into a sketch
  • Hold the right mouse button for a tool wheel

Sketching in the user manual

Parts built one feature at a time

Every boss, cut, fillet and pattern is a step in the feature tree. While you set one up, RazorCAD rolls the part back and previews it: yellow for material added, red for material removed.

  • Extrude, revolve, sweep and loft, as bosses or cuts
  • Fillet, chamfer, shell, draft, rib, Hole, threads and cosmetic threads
  • Linear, circular, curve and sketch driven patterns, and mirror
  • Several bodies per part, with Combine, Split and Move/Copy
  • Configurations and design tables driven by global variables
  • Mass properties from each body’s material
  • Millimetres or inches, chosen for each part

Solid features in the user manual

Sheet metal, with the flat pattern ready to cut

Design folded parts the way they’re made. Every sheet metal body keeps its flat pattern up to date, using your K-factor or a gauge table, and exports it to DXF with its bend lines and bend notes.

  • Base, edge and miter flanges, hems, jogs and sketched bends
  • Transitions and reducers: square, rectangle or polygon to round, cones and lofted bends
  • Convert a solid, or a surface curved one way, into sheet metal
  • Corner reliefs, closed corners, tabs and slots, dimples, louvers and extruded holes
  • Sheet Metal Check flags short flanges and holes too close to a bend

Sheet metal in the user manual

Surfaces for the shapes solids can’t make

Build zero-thickness surfaces through curves, trim and knit them together, then thicken them into a solid or let them close up into one. The same tools tidy up imported parts.

  • Extruded, revolved, lofted, swept, boundary, filled, ruled and planar surfaces
  • Offset, trim, extend, untrim, knit and thicken
  • Delete or replace faces to repair or simplify imported models
  • Split lines, projected and composite curves
  • Zebra stripes, curvature and draft analysis, and Flatten Surface to unroll a face

Surfaces in the user manual

3D sketches and welded frames

Draw lines, arcs and splines straight in space. Lines snap along X, Y and Z, and 3D relations and dimensions hold them in place, just as in a 2D sketch. Use them as sweep paths, pipe and duct routes, or the centre lines of a frame.

  • Structural members along every line: SHS, RHS, pipe, angle, channel, I-beams and bar, in AS/NZS and EN/ISO sizes
  • Corners mitred or butted, T-joints trimmed, and pipes coped automatically
  • End caps, gussets and weld beads
  • A cut list with lengths, end angles and mass, exported to CSV

Gears and airfoils, drawn for you

Some profiles are tedious to draw by hand, so RazorCAD generates them inside the sketch, where they stay editable.

  • Involute gear trains on centre lines: set the module, pressure angle, teeth or ratio, backlash and bore, and RazorCAD checks that every pair meshes
  • Airfoils on a chord line: 38 NACA 4- and 5-digit sections and Clark Y, or your own parameters. Dimension the chord and the airfoil scales with it

Gears in the user manual

Drawings for the workshop

Make a drawing from a part or an assembly and RazorCAD lays out the three standard views for you, in first or third angle projection. Add the views and notes you need, then export a PDF or DXF.

  • Projected, section, detail, auxiliary, broken and cropped views
  • Dimensions, hole callouts, cut lists and sheet metal flat patterns
  • Auto Dimension, and a drawing check for missing sizes and title block entries
  • Balloons, bill-of-materials tables and exploded views of assemblies
  • Your own templates, title blocks, logos and fonts
  • Export any flat face as a DXF for laser or waterjet cutting

Drawings in the user manual

Assemblies and the files you already have

Put parts together with mates, including gear and rack-and-pinion mates, nest sub-assemblies, explode them, watch them move in motion studies, and catch parts running into each other as you drag. Add bolts, nuts and washers from the standard parts library, drill holes through a whole stack with the fasteners put in for you, size parts together with shared equations, and list it all in a bill of materials with mass and cost. STEP assemblies open and save with their structure and colours, and big assemblies reopen in seconds.

Why RazorCAD exists

I’m a mechanical engineer, not a professional programmer. I couldn’t find an open-source CAD program that suited the way I work, so I started building one, with a lot of help from open-source libraries and Claude. My aim is a capable alternative to expensive CAD, subscriptions and paywalls, for makers and professionals alike. Built-in CAM and FEA are on the list for later.

Found a bug or missing a feature? Open an issue on GitHub.

Common questions

Is RazorCAD really free?

Yes. There’s no subscription, licence key or account, and you can use it for commercial work. RazorCAD is open source under the GPL-3.0, and the parts, assemblies and drawings you make with it are yours.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. RazorCAD runs entirely on your PC and works offline. It looks on GitHub once a day for a newer version, which you can turn off, and the setup program only goes online to fetch the Microsoft WebView2 runtime or .NET Framework if Windows is missing them.

Does it run on Mac or Linux?

RazorCAD is a Windows program for Windows 10 and 11. On a Mac or Linux PC you can try it in your browser: the same program, without auto-save or the Windows fonts.

Can I open files from other CAD programs?

Yes, through STEP, IGES, STL and DXF. STEP assemblies open with their structure and colours, IGES surfaces are sewn into solids, STL meshes become solid bodies, and DXF files import into a sketch or onto a drawing. RazorCAD also saves STEP (parts and assemblies), STL, DXF and PDF.

What does it use for geometry?

The OpenCascade B-rep kernel, the same engine behind FreeCAD, so STEP files come in and go out as exact solids rather than meshes. In the Windows app it runs multi-threaded, sized to your PC’s processor and memory, and a run of cuts is made in one go, so big parts rebuild in seconds.

Is it ready for production work?

It’s a beta, so expect some bugs. Check exported DXF files before you cut anything, and report problems on GitHub.

Install RazorCAD

  1. Download the setup program from the latest GitHub release.
  2. Run it. Install just for you, without administrator rights, or for everyone on the PC. Setup adds the Microsoft WebView2 runtime and .NET Framework 4.8 if Windows doesn’t have them yet.
  3. Open RazorCAD from the Start menu and try the sample part, or start a new one.

Running setup again updates RazorCAD. Your parts, assemblies and drawings are never touched.
